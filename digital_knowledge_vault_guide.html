Plain-English decision guide

Choosing a system for your digital knowledge vault

Your material lives in several forms: web links, video transcripts, personal notes, Google Docs, and Google Sheets. The goal is not only to store these sources, but to find connections and turn them into useful new documents or HTML pages without losing where the information came from.

A sensible starting point: keep original files in their current trusted homes (such as Google Drive), create a curated index and your own synthesis notes in a wiki-like tool such as Obsidian, and use an AI assistant to draft from selected sources. Add a custom app or vector search only after you know what the manual workflow cannot do.

Think of it as a pipeline, not one magic product

1

Collect

Keep links, transcripts, Docs, Sheets, and notes findable.

2

Describe

Add a title, topic, date, source, and short note about each item.

3

Find

Search by words, links, metadata, or meaning as needed.

4

Synthesize

Ask an LLM to draft using relevant, selected sources.

5

Review and publish

Check citations, edit, then save as a note, Doc, or HTML.

Available options

Google Drive + Docs/Sheets Keeps documents and spreadsheets in familiar formats with sharing and collaboration. Cross-file discovery and building a connected personal wiki may need extra structure or tools. Your sources already live in Google Workspace and collaboration matters.
AI notebook over selected sources Lets you ask questions and draft summaries grounded in a chosen set of sources, depending on the product and supported formats. Usually not a complete long-term filing system; verify which file types, links, and live updates it supports. Quickly exploring a project or a bundle of source materials without building software.
Obsidian or another personal wiki Creates durable, linkable notes in a human-readable format; useful for connecting ideas and writing your own synthesis. Importing, syncing, and AI extraction may require manual steps, plugins, or separate services. You want personal control, Markdown notes, and an evolving map of your knowledge.
Hosted wiki or knowledge base Provides structured pages, categories, collaboration, and often permissions. More platform dependence; migration, AI features, integrations, and data policies vary. A team needs a shared, maintained reference library.
Custom LLM web app with search Can connect sources, extract text, search across them, cite passages, and generate documents or HTML in a tailored workflow. Requires engineering, source integrations, security, evaluation, monitoring, and ongoing maintenance. You have repeated workflows or scale requirements that existing tools do not meet.

What does a vector database add?

A vector database stores numerical representations of text (embeddings) and can find passages with similar meaning, even when they do not use the same keywords. For example, a search for “ways to avoid burnout” might find a transcript that says “protecting energy and preventing exhaustion.”

A vector database is a search component, not a complete knowledge system. It does not automatically connect to Google Drive, understand every file, create a reliable wiki, or guarantee a correct answer. You still need source access, text extraction, permissions, citations, and a way to review generated documents.

  • Start with normal file organization and full-text search if your collection is modest and keyword search works.
  • Consider vector search when you repeatedly need concept-based retrieval across many documents or transcripts.
  • Consider a hybrid search (keywords plus vectors and metadata filters) when exact names, dates, and semantic concepts all matter.

Keep source material separate from your knowledge and outputs

A healthy vault distinguishes what someone else originally said from your own notes and from AI-generated drafts. That makes corrections, updates, and source checks much easier. 

Knowledge workspace/
├── Sources/
│   ├── Links/
│   ├── Video-transcripts/
│   ├── Google-Docs/
│   └── Google-Sheets/
├── Notes/
│   ├── Topics/
│   └── People-and-projects/
├── Drafts/
│   ├── Reports/
│   └── HTML/
└── Index/
    └── Start-here.md

This is a conceptual layout; Google Docs and Sheets can remain in Drive. Your index or wiki can store links and descriptions rather than duplicate every source file.

Give each source a small amount of structure

Even a lightweight catalog makes later search and AI work more reliable. You can use frontmatter in Markdown, columns in a Sheet, or fields in a wiki. 

---
title: "Interview about sustainable design"
source_type: video_transcript
source_url: "https://example.com/source"
date_added: 2026-10-30
topics:
  - design
  - sustainability
status: reviewed
---

# Short note
One or two sentences describing why this source matters.

For a Google Sheet, the same idea could be columns for title, source type, URL, date, topics, summary, and review status. Keep source links and access permissions intact.

How AI can help create new documents

  1. Choose a question or deliverable, such as “Create a short guide to the ideas I collected about sustainable design.”
  2. Find the relevant notes and sources. Do not send the entire vault if only a few sources are needed.
  3. Ask the model to draft a specific structure and distinguish source facts from your conclusions.
  4. Require links or citations for important claims, ideally pointing to the original document and a relevant page, section, or transcript timestamp.
  5. Review the draft against the sources, correct mistakes, and save it as a new document or HTML page.
  6. Record which sources and prompt produced the draft so it can be refreshed later.

Example prompt

Create a plain-English HTML guide about [topic] using only the source notes below.

Requirements:
- Organize it with a short introduction, key ideas, practical steps, and open questions.
- Cite each important claim with the supplied source title and link.
- Separate what the sources say from any synthesis or recommendation.
- If the sources disagree or do not answer something, say so.
- Do not invent facts, quotations, links, or citations.

Source notes:
{{selected notes and source links}}

Choosing a path for your situation

If this is mainly for you

Keep originals in Drive, make a simple catalog, and build topic notes in Obsidian or another Markdown wiki. Use AI on selected sources, then save reviewed output back as linked notes or HTML.

If you already work mostly in Google

Start with Drive folders and a catalog Sheet, then use an AI feature that can access the specific sources you choose. Confirm format support and data access before relying on it for Docs, Sheets, or external links.

If a team needs a shared reference

Consider a collaborative wiki or knowledge base with clear owners, review dates, permissions, and a publishing process. AI drafts can help, but somebody should own keeping canonical pages current.

If you need a tailored automated product

Build a custom app only after defining source connectors, user access, output formats, citations, and quality checks. Add vector search if semantic retrieval is a proven need, not simply because the app uses an LLM.

A low-risk plan to get started

  1. Pick one topic and gather 10–20 representative sources from your vault.
  2. Create a catalog with a stable title, source link, type, topic, and short description.
  3. Write a topic note that links to those sources and captures your own understanding.
  4. Use an AI assistant to draft one document from only those sources.
  5. Check every important claim and link; save the reviewed version as a new file.
  6. Note what was difficult: finding sources, importing content, citations, or formatting. Let that evidence determine what you automate next.

Recommendation: start with a hybrid personal wiki plus your existing Google Drive, not a custom app or vector database. Keep source files where they are, build a curated index and connected notes, and use an LLM as a drafting assistant with source links. Upgrade to automation or semantic search when you can point to a specific recurring pain that the simpler setup does not solve.

Questions to answer before choosing

  • Is this only for you, or will other people need access and editing rights?
  • Should original files stay in Google Drive, or do you want local Markdown copies?
  • Do you need answers with source citations, or mainly a place to write and connect ideas?
  • How often should changes in Docs and Sheets be reflected in your knowledge notes?
  • Can private or personal material be sent to a cloud AI provider?
  • Who will review AI-created content and keep the final documents up to date?
In brief: choose a wiki for organizing and connecting knowledge, an AI assistant for drafting from sources, and a vector database only when semantic search is needed at meaningful scale. Keep originals, citations, and human review in the workflow.
